# CRM Selection for Marketing Analysts: Don’t Ignore Tracking

A CRM decision is not only a sales decision. For marketing analysts, the CRM often becomes the bridge between website behavior, form submissions, campaign attribution, lead quality, and closed revenue.

Before choosing a CRM, teams should understand how the platform handles forms, UTMs, hidden fields, lifecycle stages, source fields, integrations, and conversion tracking.

## What sales teams usually care about

Sales teams usually care about pipeline visibility, contact records, follow-up tasks, deal stages, ownership, and ease of use.

## What marketing analysts need to verify

Marketing analysts need to know whether the CRM can support source tracking, campaign attribution, form capture, lifecycle reporting, and closed-loop measurement.

## Why hidden fields matter

Hidden fields can carry useful attribution data into the CRM, including campaign parameters, conversion IDs, page context, and other tracking values.

## CRM selection criteria should include measurement

A CRM may look good in a demo, but if it makes attribution difficult, the business may struggle to understand which campaigns, channels, and forms are creating qualified opportunities.

## Bottom line

The right CRM is not just the one with the best sales interface. It is the one that supports the business’s real operating needs: adoption, reporting, attribution, and revenue visibility.
